My employer has a history of curious naming calamities. Read on to discover how I'm the worst offender.

---

### NeXT, neXt, NExT

The convention employed in our x-Series is a lowercase "x" be in the product name.
This was the first to deviate from a leading "x." To this day, people can't seem to
get this straight.

### What the flex

In the early days, these were marketed as flexible. They used a wound wire antenna thermal compression welded to transponders. Time showed the less the moved the better. A pivot to flex pcb made them more reliable in some ways and more delicate in others. "Flex" has become more of a
reference to the material since the advise is to avoid areas of flexion.

### x-Series

The expectation for our customers is that these injectable, bioglass implants. The reality of our label--casting aside the material lessons of
flex naming--is that x-Series are _any_ injectable implants. As seen by the VivoKey Thermo and xG3 v1r.

### Bioresin

Okay, so this category is literally just a material name so it should contain all implants made from our bioresin, correct? Wrong. These are
essentially the successor to the flex category. It turns out its less of a material thing and more of a size/shape/installability thing. This
category faetures implants such as the dUG4T while side stepping the injectables and magnets that also use our bioresin.

### It's not just us

What exactly is a Lodestone? It seems to be a name applied to both biomagnets and peripherials that use them. Lodestone #### = biomagnet. Lodestone Pico = haptic peripherial. Lodestone Mouse = haptic peripherial. Don't get me wrong, this is a sequencing issue, I feel. Naming the biomagnets lodestones seems to jive more than applying it to peripherials but the peripherials came first.
